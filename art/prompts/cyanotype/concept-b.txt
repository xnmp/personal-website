Art direction "Cyanotype": a botanist-engineer's sun-print archive. Behind everything, a wall of overlapping cyanotype photograms in deep Prussian blue, each the white silhouette of a real object: fern fronds and grasses, a mechanical keyboard, clock gears, a circuit board, a pair of dividers, pinned edge to edge and catching soft daylight from the upper left, the centre a calm stretch of plain blue print.

The content panels are archival photo mounts: thick white museum board with a faint paper tooth and crisp straight edges, each held to the wall by translucent paper photo corners at its four corners. Type on the mounts is printed in deep Prussian-blue ink. Buttons are paper labels: small rectangles of off-white card with a thin printed blue rule border, typed in blue; the primary button is a label printed solid Prussian blue with white type, like a small cyanotype. Screens are set into the mounts behind a bevel-cut window. Status markers are small round paper dots (blue, yellow, a pale green, a soft red). Labels are typed on narrow strips of cream paper tape. Headings in an elegant engraved serif.

Palette: Prussian blue #1f4e8c and its deep #12325e, museum white #f7f6f1, cream tape #efe6cf, sun yellow #f2c14e for status only, blue-black ink #142033. Soft daylight from the upper left. Crisp, scientific, handmade, expensive; generous space; a AAA game menu, not a flat website. Placeholder text only as abstract lines except the large headline "Tools I use every day".
