Do not copy its style.

Art direction "Cyanotype": a sun-printing studio. Behind everything, one enormous cyanotype photogram on rough watercolour paper, deep Prussian blue, with the white silhouettes of real objects laid on it when it was exposed: fern fronds at the left, a mechanical keyboard and a scatter of keycaps at the right, clock gears and a pair of compasses near the bottom, the blue graded where the objects lifted a little from the paper, the centre open blue with a few faint brush marks of the sensitiser.

The content panels are cyanotype prints themselves: sheets of heavy white watercolour paper brushed with Prussian-blue cyanotype, the brushed emulsion stopping a finger's width from the paper's edge in loose, dry brush strokes so a ragged white margin of bare paper frames each blue panel, held to the background with short strips of cream paper tape across two corners. Type on the panels is white, as if printed by a stencil. Buttons are paper labels: small rectangles of off-white card with a thin printed rule border, typed in dark blue; the primary button is a label of warm sun-yellow card. Screens are mounted in white card window mounts with a bevelled cut edge. Status markers are small round paper dots (white, yellow, a pale green, a soft red). Labels are typed on narrow strips of cream paper tape. Headings in an elegant engraved serif.

Palette: Prussian blue #1f4e8c and its deep #12325e, white paper #f6f4ee, cream tape #efe6cf, sun yellow #f2c14e (the one accent), blue-black ink #142033. Soft daylight from the upper left. Crisp, scientific, handmade, expensive; generous space; a AAA game menu, not a flat website. Placeholder text only as abstract lines except the large headline "Tools I use every day".
