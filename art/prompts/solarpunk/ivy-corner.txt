A sheet of four separate sprigs of ivy on a fully transparent background (real alpha, no backdrop), arranged in a 2x2 grid with wide empty gaps between them so that no sprig touches another or the edge of the canvas. Painted like the ivy that grows over the brass picture frames in the reference image (ref.png): heart-shaped (cordate) leaves, rounded lobes and a short pointed tip, on thin wiry stems, in a rich mid-green and deep green with warm yellow-green where the low golden sun catches their edges, a natural satin sheen as of sunlit leaves in a painted photograph, veins faint. Each leaf is about one eighteenth of the canvas width, and all four sprigs use leaves of that size (a few smaller ones near the tips).
Each sprig is a corner overgrowth, as seen on the top right corner of a picture frame: a dense clump at the upper right of its quadrant, made of twelve to sixteen overlapping heart-shaped leaves of varied sizes pointing in all directions; from the clump a thin vine runs LEFT along an invisible horizontal line, carrying five to eight leaves spaced well apart (sparse, a leaf here and there); and from the clump a second thin vine hangs straight DOWN along an invisible vertical line at the right, carrying six to nine leaves spaced apart, getting smaller toward its tip. So each sprig is an upside-down L with its thick elbow at the upper right. The four sprigs clearly differ from each other: the first has a long top vine and a short side vine, the second a short top vine and a long side vine, the third a very dense elbow with a sparse thin tail each way, the fourth two small clumps on its top vine and an even side vine.
NO specular highlights, NO sparkle, NO glints, NO white highlight dots, NO dew, NO wet glossy blobs. No frame, no wall, no floor, no background, no cast shadow: only the ivy on transparency. Square canvas, at the highest resolution available.
