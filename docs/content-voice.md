# Writing project pages

Use the owner's saved `content/about.md` and hand-written Tauri Explorer opening
as the voice references. These are notes from a technical person's life and
experiments. Keep them short enough to leave room for the owner to fill them in.

- Start with the actual interest, task or annoyance. Specific details such as
  pressing Enter twice are useful; generic product positioning is not.
- Keep supplied phrasing, humour and uncertainty. Lightly edit for readability;
  don't imitate informality by adding slang or invented anecdotes.
- First-person history needs a source: the owner's words, existing personal
  notes or an explicit account in the repository. Features alone don't establish
  why somebody built them or what they felt.
- Describe technical choices where they explain a problem, decision or result.
  Keep dated experiments and their limits together. Test counts and lines of
  code aren't credentials; repeated declarations of honesty aren't a story.
- Use modest, literal headings and links. Avoid slogans, recruitment funnels,
  superiority claims and feature inventories dressed up as prose.
- Keep each page distinct. A diary rewrite, an old game and a query compiler
  don't need the same narrative template or a compulsory moral at the end.
- Keep each section's prose in one editable Markdown block, including its
  paragraphs, lists and small subheadings. Do not split it into paragraph-sized
  fields when revising copy. Prefer a brief factual starting point
  over filling a gap with imagined personal history or public placeholders.

Tauri Explorer has its own product website. Its page here is for the personal
story and build notes, with an obvious link to that website. Product marketing,
downloads and onboarding belong there.
